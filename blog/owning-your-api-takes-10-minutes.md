## The Lie That Keeps Agencies Dependent

Somewhere along the way, agencies absorbed a quiet belief:

**“APIs are technical.”**

That single sentence has created a decade of dependency.

Because if APIs are technical…  
then only developers can touch them.  
And if only developers can touch them…  
then agencies must let vendors handle everything.

Which is exactly what vendors want.

**Business control.**

## What Owning Your API Actually Means

Here is the full process for creating a Meta developer app:

**Step 1:** Go to developers.facebook.com  
**Step 2:** Click “Create App”  
**Step 3:** Choose “Business” type  
**Step 4:** Name it (e.g., “[Your Agency] Publishing”)  
**Step 5:** Add Pages API + Instagram API  
**Step 6:** Submit for review  

**Time required:** 10–15 minutes  
**Technical skills required:** Ability to fill out a form  

That’s it.

No backend.  
No servers.  
No developers.

Just administrative setup.

## The Apartment vs. Estate Analogy

When your client clicks **“Connect account”**:

**Rented Tool Model**  
Your client authorizes the vendor’s app.  
You watch.

**Agency-Owned Model**  
Your client authorizes your agency’s app.  
You own.

Same button.  
Same flow.  
Completely different ownership.

When your client clicks “Connect,” they are not shaking hands with you.

They are shaking hands with your vendor.

You’re standing in the hallway.

## Vendor Model vs. Agency-Owned Model

| Dimension | Vendor Model | Agency-Owned Model |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------|
| Client authorizes | "Hootsuite" | "[Your Agency] Publishing" |
| Tokens stored in | Vendor database | Your infrastructure |
| If you switch tools | Re-auth all clients | Tokens move with you |
| If vendor has outage | You go down too | Only your UI affected |
| Cost structure | Per-seat pricing | Flat infrastructure fee |
| Who owns access | Vendor | You |

This is not cosmetic.

This is structural.
